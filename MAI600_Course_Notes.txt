Section 2 - Academic Details

2.1 Overview of this unit

This unit on Advanced Machine and Deep Learning Applications provides students with a comprehensive exploration of cutting-edge techniques and methodologies that are driving innovation across various domains. Beginning with an overview of advanced machine learning concepts, students recap foundational algorithms before delving into the powerful realm of deep learning and neural networks. They gain insights into the pivotal role these advanced methods play in modern applications, from computer vision and natural language processing to generative models and reinforcement learning.

Students will learn about feedforward neural networks, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for computer vision tasks, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for sequential data, generative models like variational autoencoders (VAEs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs), and attention mechanisms and transformers for sequence-to-sequence modelling. The unit further covers advanced topics such as reinforcement learning, transfer learning, meta-learning, and interpretability in AI. Through group projects and case studies spanning diverse domains, students gain hands-on experience in applying these advanced techniques to real-world challenges, reinforcing theoretical concepts with practical applications and preparing them to excel as machine learning experts.

2.6 Teaching methods

This unit will be delivered on campus and online over 4 hours per week. Where appropriate, assessments will be submitted to Turnitin for authentication checking. The 4R/5R reflective framework (Report, Respond, Relate, Reason, Reconstruct) will be explicitly taught and modelled before reflective assessments. Students will be guided to move beyond description by critically evaluating their contribution, learning, teamwork, challenges, alternative approaches and future professional application, supported by relevant academic literature where required. A1 is progressive across Sessions 2, 4, 6 and 8, with separate Canvas evidence/submission points and formative feedback provided during the teaching period.

Academic integrity and referencing

Maintaining high standards of academic integrity requires that students have access to guidance and support on how to access and cite information correctly. ASA has developed academic integrity induction materials for online access by staff and students, with resources on appropriate academic writing skills and referencing. Students are encouraged to practice academic integrity and any breaches will not be tolerated including plagiarism, collusion, contract cheating, and overuse of AI without referencing.

Academic integrity means upholding high ethical standards of academic conduct. Plagiarism means the unattributed use of another writer's words, ideas and arguments as one's own. Instances of plagiarism and other forms of academic misconduct will be addressed within the framework of ASA's Policy and Procedure covering the topic of Academic Integrity.

All ASA students are expected to complete an online Academic Integrity Module prior to starting their first study period. Students are expected to receive a result of 100% in the module. Students will have as many attempts as possible to receive this result. The Academic Integrity Module will be accessible to all students throughout their studies with ASA if they wish to go back and to refresh their understanding or check on rules and regulations.

Some students against whom allegations of academic misconduct are brought often say that they did not know or understand the meaning of misconduct or that if they did know and understand that they were simply careless or under pressure. ASA expects students to be confident that they know and understand the ASA's definition of Student Misconduct and are aware of the penalties if allegations of misconduct are proven.

Allegations of student misconduct are taken seriously by ASA and if proven, can lead to penalties that may depend on whether a student is a first, second- or third-time offender. It is the responsibility of the student to read and understand the policies relevant to Academic Integrity and the consequences for breaching those policies. If in doubt, it is better to ask if the students are confused and check with your unit coordinator and or lecturer.

Students must attach an assessment cover sheet to all submitted assessments, with a signed declaration of originality.

The American Psychological Association (APA 7th ed.) referencing style is used throughout this course.

Special Consideration and late submissions

Assessment items submitted after the assessment due date without an approved extension for mitigating circumstances may be penalised. The standard penalty is the reduction of the mark allocated to the assessment item by 5% of the total mark applicable for the assessment item for the first working day or part working day, and then 10% for each working day or part working day that the item is late (a 'working day' for this purpose is defined as any day on which institute administration is open). Assessment items submitted more than ten working days after the assessment due date is awarded zero marks.

Students may apply for special consideration for an assessment using the Special Consideration Request Form if they experience sickness or other circumstances outside their control that adversely affect their ability to study. The decision shall be at the discretion of the Dean and will be granted in writing.
